## Day 9 : Bali Zoo

_Date: Saturday, Jun 21, 2025_

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/9_1.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Common Area at Junna House
  </figcaption>
</figure>

We had a lazy morning (shocking for us, I know) because honestly, we’d built in these two buffer days just in case we felt worn out. After breakfast, we bid a fond farewell to Junna House — a stay I truly wish we could’ve extended.  
We had only two sights penciled in: **[Bali Zoo](https://maps.app.goo.gl/o7U34vpX2W49U6DPA)** and **[Tegenungan Waterfall](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mbVRLWcACPemy7KS7)**. The Bali Zoo was about a 40-minute drive (traffic depending), so that’s where we headed first.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/9_2.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Selfie at Junna House
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Bali Zoo is located in Sukawati, Gianyar Regency, spanning about 22 acres of tropical gardens, and houses over 350 to 450 animals including species like orangutans, Sumatran elephants, and Bengal tigers. It offers interactive programs like “Breakfast with Orangutans,” animal feedings, and petting zones.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/9_3.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Selfie at Bali Zoo
  </figcaption>
</figure>

To be honest, the zoo left me slightly underwhelmed. The enclosures felt a bit tight, and many animals seemed lethargic (perhaps habituated to constant visitors). After about an hour of wandering, fatigue from our earlier days of non-stop exploring caught up to us. We decided to pause for lunch and then revisit whether we’d push onward to **[Tegenungan Waterfall](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mbVRLWcACPemy7KS7)**.

Kusuma guided us to a surprise — a pure vegetarian restaurant, **[Hare Krishna Vegetarian](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QrnDAQ5QsARfvPDq7)**. Finding such a place in Bali (where non-veg reigns) felt lucky. We learned the restaurant is run by devotees of Krishna who maintain strict vegetarianism. We ordered local-style vegetarian renditions, and the meal was satisfying and wholesome.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md=w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/9_4.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Food at Hare Krishna Vegetarian
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After lunch, we decided to gently throw in the towel and just rest. We were beat. So we headed to our next hotel for the remaining two nights: **[Grand Barong Resort](https://maps.app.goo.gl/R1mUpCY9bJJwx8iH7)** in Kuta. The roads approaching it were so narrow that at first I wondered if the photos online were fake — but no, the place was exactly as promised. This was the most commercial (read: bustling) stay of the trip: right beside a busy road leading to Kuta Beach.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/9_5.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    The ladies at Kuta Beach
  </figcaption>
</figure>

We checked in, rested a bit, then headed to **Kuta Beach**. Expectations met reality — crowded, competitive for space, and very commercial. We couldn’t find a decent sitting spot, so we simply stood, watched surfers, and even glimpsed planes landing in the distance (random but cool).

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/9_6.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Busy streets of Kuta
  </figcaption>
</figure>

We picked up some essentials at a nearby supermarket, then retreated back to the hotel. Dinner was low-key, and we chilled by the pool until bedtime.

## Day 10 : Souvenir Shopping

_Date: Sunday, Jun 22, 2025_

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/10_1.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Kuta Beach
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This day was purely about souvenirs (for me) — my family had mostly done theirs already, but I had to pick stuff for friends back home - it was my first international trip after all. After a slow start, we used Google Maps to scout clusters of souvenir markets, then set off.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/10_2.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    I don't know why do they sell these or who even buys it!? 😭
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Whoa, bargaining 101 in Bali is real. Every vendor quoted prices at least 10×–20× what they probably expected, so we haggled hard. According to Kusuma, even after our bargain, the sellers still made a good margin — so we didn’t feel too guilty 😝. Lesson: if you're going to Bali and shopping souvenirs, **bargain hard**!

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/10_3.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Selfie at Kuta Beach
  </figcaption>
</figure>

We walked (and haggled) until we got hangry. Then we dashed back to the hotel, dumped our loot, and made our way to a nearby Indian restaurant: **[Shiv Shakti](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HdcNHqmbwvRD8p727)**. The restaurant is run by an Indian living in Kuta. The food was fantastic — comforting in a foreign place — and portions generous. Highly recommended if you need a taste of home.

Post-lunch, we returned to the hotel to recharge. My two aunts headed back for more souvenir hunting; Mom and I opted for some quiet hotel downtime.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-9-10-11/10_4.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    I forgot to click photos of our stay so this is all I got! 😭
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Dinner was from the last of our supermarket stash, and we sat chatting about this being our first international trip (for me, Mom, and one aunt). With heavy hearts, we drifted to sleep — the trip was officially over!

## Day 11 : And it’s a wrap!

_Date: Monday, Jun 23, 2025_

Our flight home was at 11:20 AM, so we started the day early. We checked out and Kusuma, ever punctual, picked us up for the final transfer.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/intro/0_1.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">

  </figcaption>

At the airport, we said our thanks and farewells to Kusuma — who by now felt less like a driver and more like a friend. And just like that, our Bali chapter closed. I can’t wait to dive into Bali again someday (literally, yes, for the waters) 😂.
