## Day 1 - Travel to Bali and Visiting the Uluwatu Temple

_Date: Friday, Jun 13, 2025_

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/1_1.png" />

We had a very early start to our day—or rather, our previous day never really ended. Our flight was scheduled for 01:15, which meant we had to leave home around 20:00 the previous evening to beat Bengaluru traffic. Thankfully, our flight took off right on time and we were set to land in Bali at 10:20 (local time).

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/1_2.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Photo from the Flight
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After landing, we went through the usual formalities, starting with our visas. For Indian passport holders, Indonesia offers Visa-on-Arrival, so there was no need for prior application. The process was a little crowded, and we made our first mistake here: paying the visa fee in USD cash. Bad idea! The counters don’t follow proper exchange rates, and we ended up losing quite a bit in conversion. A forex card would’ve saved us money—lesson learned!

I had pre-booked our entire itinerary with our driver, Kusuma—an incredibly friendly guy who made our journey smooth and comfortable throughout. Before heading out, I also picked up a SIM card from the airport. Another mistake! Kusuma later told me that SIMs inside the airport are sold at a premium. It would’ve been better to use the airport WiFi, contact him, and buy one outside at a local vendor. Another small lesson to note down for future trips.

Kusuma greeted us at the airport with refreshing tender coconut water. Our first stop was a currency exchange center. I had carried USD from India, since converting INR directly to IDR is usually more expensive. After exchanging some money (and realizing how huge the IDR notes look when stacked!), and paying Kusuma his advance, we drove to our stay at **[Casa Asia](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3cMVZusJ87jAi5xB9)**—a peaceful and charming place.

We were starving by then, having skipped breakfast due to the early flight. Luckily, Casa Asia had an attached restaurant with great reviews. The food lived up to the praise and was absolutely delightful! After lunch, we took a short nap before heading to our first sightseeing spot—**[Uluwatu Temple](https://maps.app.goo.gl/C4Bv3irP6Rm1HXRH6)**.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/1_3.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Casa Asia
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Uluwatu Temple, also known as Pura Luhur Uluwatu, is one of Bali’s six key sea temples and sits majestically on top of a steep cliff, about 70 meters above the ocean. Dedicated to Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa (the Supreme God) in his manifestation as Rudra, it’s not just a spiritual site but also one of the best places to witness Bali’s famous sunsets over the Indian Ocean. The temple is also known for its resident monkeys and the traditional Kecak Fire Dance performed at sunset.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/1_4.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Group photo at Uluwatu Temple
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Oh boy, Uluwatu Temple was quite an experience—both good and bad! On the good side, the temple is breathtakingly beautiful and well-maintained despite the large number of visitors. The cliffside views are stunning and picture-perfect.

On the other hand, the crowds were heavy, especially when lining up for the Kecak Dance amphitheater. But the real challenge was the monkeys. These little troublemakers are notorious for snatching belongings—phones, sunglasses, even spectacles—sometimes just to destroy them. We were cautious, keeping everything packed away, but towards the evening we slipped up. Thinking the monkeys had disappeared in the dark, my aunt put her spectacles back on. Out of nowhere, one darted in, grabbed them, and began tearing them apart! Nothing we could do. Thankfully, she had a spare pair back at our stay.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/1_5.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Uluwatu Temple
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After that ordeal, we settled in for the famous Kecak Dance. The Kecak Dance is one of Bali’s most iconic cultural performances. Performed at sunset against the backdrop of Uluwatu’s cliffs, it involves a large male chorus chanting “cak-cak-cak” in unison, creating a rhythmic soundtrack like an acapella. The performance usually enacts episodes from the Ramayana, with fire elements adding drama. Unlike most Balinese dances, it has no musical instruments—only the human voice and chanting. The performance was energetic, powerful, and deeply engaging—an amazing way to end our first day in Bali.

  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">

  </figcaption>
</figure>

We stopped at a nearby Indian restaurant for dinner on our way back to Casa Asia and called it a night. Rest was much needed after such a packed start.

## Day 2 - Visiting the Bajra Sandhi Monument and the Garuda Wisnu Kencana Park

_Date: Saturday, Jun 14, 2025_

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_1.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Ganesha at Casa Asia
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After a restful night, we enjoyed a relaxed morning at Casa Asia. My aunt had packed some ready-to-eat Indian mixes (Poha and Upma), which she prepared for breakfast, along with instant coffee. After a cozy start to the day, we got ready for our first stop—the **[Bajra Sandhi Monument](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jN6rQbGuGTE3tbFD9)** in Denpasar, about 40 minutes (25 km) away. Kusuma, ever punctual, was waiting for us by the gate.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_2.gif" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Bali Mandara Toll Road
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Our route took us along the Bali Mandara Toll Road, a beautiful bridge built over the sea connecting Nusa Dua to Denpasar. The drive was scenic, with calm morning views of the ocean. Soon, we reached the Bajra Sandhi Monument.

The Bajra Sandhi Monument is a striking landmark built to honor the struggles of the Balinese people throughout history. Shaped like a traditional Balinese bell used by priests (bajra), it symbolizes resilience and spirituality. Inside, dioramas narrate Bali’s journey through colonization, wars, and the fight for independence. It serves as both a museum and a cultural monument.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_3.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Entrance to Bajra Sandhi Monument
  </figcaption>
</figure>

There were groups of schoolchildren on picnics around the park, which reminded me of my own school days. Since most tourists in Bali start their day late, we had the place almost to ourselves—quiet, peaceful, and beautifully maintained. We took our time reading the exhibits describing Bali’s hardships under Dutch and Japanese rule—eerily similar to India’s pre-independence struggles.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_4.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">

We then climbed to the upper floors, where the views were stunning.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_5.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">

After some relaxed chatting and clicking pictures, we asked Kusuma to pick us up for our next stop—the **[Werdhi Budaya Art Centre](https://maps.app.goo.gl/y16SAeQvFYq5JdN2A)**. This was more of a filler stop. Occasionally, performances are held here, and once a year a large cultural festival takes place. But otherwise, there wasn’t much happening. We browsed a few art shops, though the prices were steep. Kusuma also advised us to save shopping for Ubud or Kuta markets, which are much more affordable. With that in mind, we soon headed back to Casa Asia for lunch, since our next big stop—GWK Cultural Park—was nearby.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_6.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Back of the GWK Statue from Casa Asia
  </figcaption>

After a hearty meal and a short nap, we got ready for the **[Garuda Wisnu Kencana (GWK) Cultural Park](https://maps.app.goo.gl/6VjqGS6iA2NVVXCW7)**.

GWK Cultural Park is one of Bali’s most iconic attractions, spread across 60 hectares. Its centerpiece is the colossal statue of Lord Vishnu riding the mythical Garuda, which at 121 meters is taller than the Statue of Liberty. The park also features smaller statues, cultural performances, and wide open plazas used for events and concerts. It’s a mix of spirituality, culture, and art on a grand scale.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_7.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Garuda & Vishnu Statue from afar
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The park involves a lot of walking. Though a buggy service is available, we chose to explore on foot. Midway, it started raining, and we found ourselves slowly walking uphill with umbrellas—something that unexpectedly felt nostalgic, reminding me of my carefree college days.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_8.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Fam in the rain 👋
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The massive Garuda–Vishnu statue is accessible inside up to certain floors, with displays and viewpoints. Since we were short on time, we explored only the lower levels, which still had plenty to offer. Afterward, we treated ourselves to some delicious gelato at the café on the ground floor. When the rain eased, we stepped out, took photos in front of the statue, and continued exploring. On our way down, we stumbled upon a traditional dance performance area, but unfortunately arrived just as the show ended. Still, the smaller statues of Vishnu and Lakshmi, along with the clearing skies, gave us a spectacular sunset view—perfect for photos.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-1-2/2_9.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Garuda and Vishnu Statues
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After all that walking, we wrapped up the day with dinner again at Casa Asia’s restaurant, now a familiar comfort. Some chit-chat later, we called it a night. An early morning awaited us for our journey to Nusa Penida!
