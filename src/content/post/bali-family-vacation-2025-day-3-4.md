## Day 3 - Cliffs, Beaches & Hidden Gems of Nusa Penida

_Date: Sunday, Jun 15, 2025_

After a good night's rest, we woke up and freshened up. We had to check out early from our hotel, Casa Asia, as we had a speedboat to Nusa Penida departing at 08:00. Since our stay was about 1–1.5 hours away from Sanur Port, we checked out by 06:30. As usual, Kusuma—our driver—was right on time! Saying goodbye to a wonderful stay at Casa Asia, we headed towards Nusa Penida.

Sanur Port was quite crowded. Thankfully, Kusuma had already pre-booked our tickets through his contacts, so we didn’t have to worry about the bookings. Although the names on the tickets didn’t match ours (xD), we figured as long as it worked, it worked! After waiting about 30 minutes, we boarded our speedboat at 08:00 and began our journey to Nusa Penida. Oh my, what a ride that was! Before coming to Bali, I had read a few news reports about speedboats here going too fast and sometimes capsizing in rough seas. I didn’t share that with my family to avoid unnecessary anxiety. But the ride was fine—bumpy but fun. After about an hour on the water, we arrived in Nusa Penida!

Nusa Penida felt very different compared to mainland Bali—it seemed less developed. The roads were narrower and bumpier, buildings looked older, and overall, it had a rustic vibe. But that’s part of its charm: dramatic cliffs, remote beaches, and a raw, natural feel.

Alit, a local driver, was waiting to pick us up. After paying a small entrance fee to the island, we headed to our first stop: **[Kelingking Beach](https://maps.app.goo.gl/7HUjs7ruaj23Bumj7)**.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/bali-family-vacation-2025/day-3-4/3_3.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Kelingking Beach - View from the Top
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Kelingking Beach is arguably the most iconic spot in Nusa Penida, famous for its cliff formation that looks like a T-Rex head jutting into the ocean. From the top, you get a breathtaking panoramic view of turquoise waters crashing against the limestone cliffs. It’s the postcard image of Nusa Penida that you’ll find everywhere—but being there in person is an entirely different experience.

The main attraction for most visitors is the viewpoint at the top, where people take photos with the T-Rex-shaped cliff in the background. If you’re not planning to trek down, 30 minutes is more than enough time here—it’s mostly just fighting for a good spot to take a photo. For me, however, one of the main reasons to visit Kelingking was to hike down to the beach itself—and that’s exactly what I did. The trek was short but intense. It took me about 50 minutes for the round trip, but the time can vary depending on your fitness and how crowded the trail is. The trail is only about 1.2 km, but it’s steep, narrow, and in some parts you need to descend/ascend almost like climbing a ladder. It was exhausting but absolutely worth it—the beach at the bottom is pristine, with crystal-clear water and soft white sand. My only regret was not having enough time to swim, as my family had stayed at the top and was waiting for me. I was drenched in sweat when I climbed back up, but the view and the experience made it completely worthwhile.

After resting a bit at the top, we decided to grab lunch since everyone was hungry. Vegetarian food options in Nusa Penida are more limited than in mainland Bali, but we managed to find a small restaurant along the way. After eating, we continued to our next destinations: **[Broken Beach](https://maps.app.goo.gl/xjQ2AGFVq4DdFuxr6)** and **[Angel’s Billabong](https://maps.app.goo.gl/uGAbb1n3PrKCQias7)**.

On our walk to Broken Beach, we spotted a very friendly, cute cat—and of course, it would’ve been a crime not to stop and pet it! So my mum and I spent a few minutes playing with it before continuing to the viewpoint.

Broken Beach (Pasih Uug) is a natural rock arch overlooking the ocean. Instead of a typical sandy beach, it’s a large circular cove where the sea has eroded the rock, creating a giant natural pool that connects to the ocean through a stone archway. It’s a striking sight, with waves constantly crashing through the arch. Right next to it is Angel’s Billabong, a natural infinity pool formed by tide pools on the rocky cliffs. When the tide is low and conditions are safe, you can even wade in and enjoy the crystal-clear water, though visitors are strongly advised to be cautious as sudden waves can be dangerous.

We spent about 45 minutes to an hour exploring both spots. After exploring, clicking some pictures, and enjoying coconuts (which turned out to be massive—two were more than enough for all of us!), we headed back to the car.

## Day 4 - From Open Water to Advanced: A Diver’s Milestone

_Date: Monday, Jun 16, 2025_

Today I had a separate plan from my family: my EAN40 (Enriched Air Nitrox) course with **[Pure Dive Resort](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1d77B9Gt5VkbY4Jx7)**. This was the last requirement for me to qualify as an Advanced Open Water Diver. I arrived at the dive shop around 09:00, completed some paperwork, and met my instructor, [Prisilia](https://www.instagram.com/pre_silly/). After a quick revision of the theory and some practical drills—like using the oxygen analyzer to test tanks and labeling them with their Nitrox content—we headed to the boat. Since the EAN40 course doesn’t require skill drills underwater, it was going to be three fun dives for me. And oh boy, what a day it turned out to be!

Our first dive site was **Manta Point**, located on the opposite side of the island. The thrill began with the boat ride itself—our captain was “full sending” it across the waves, giving us brief moments of airtime xD. After a bumpy 30–40 minute ride, we reached Manta Point. The dive lasted about 55 minutes, with a maximum depth of 23.3 m and an average depth of 15.5 m but the dive was nothing short of magical: crystal-clear water with 20m+ visibility, vibrant corals, and most importantly—majestic manta rays! This was my first time seeing manta rays; one circled gracefully around me for minutes, and soon more appeared—two, then three together. It felt like a dream come true.

The second dive was at **Pura Ped**, a sloping coral wall that drops away into deep, dark water—Prisilia mentioned the wall goes beyond 100 m. This site was a drift dive, so the current steadily carried us along the reef.

I’ve been on a weight-loss journey and, with improved fitness and roughly 42 dives under my belt, my buoyancy control had gotten better and I’d reduced the lead on my belt—from around 6 kg when I first started to about 2 kg now. Still, on the previous dive at Manta Point I’d struggled with buoyancy and found myself sinking more than I wanted (probably from excitement after seeing mantas), which made Pura Ped feel especially daunting.

At Pura Ped the current felt slightly diagonal and downward, constantly tugging toward the depths. Being a little overweighted combined with that diagonal pull kept me fighting to stay neutral, and it was easy to feel anxious about sinking too deep—especially since we were on Nitrox, which limits allowable depths. The extra weight, the current, and the steep wall made the dive physically tiring, even though the reef was spectacular.

Despite the challenge, it was an amazing experience: the dive lasted about 46 minutes, with a maximum depth of 29.1 m and an average depth of 16.8 m, and it was full of colorful corals and the unique thrill of my first drift dive.

After the dive, we stopped at a local warung for lunch, where I had one of the best Mi Gorengs of my trip—shoutout to [Warung Putu Ariyantini](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MWE1ARZwZ2rty68H6)! Then it was time for the third and final dive of the day: **Toyapakeh** (which Kusuma later told me literally means “Salt Water”). This time, I corrected my weighting mistake, reducing my belt from 2kg to just 1kg—and the difference was massive! My buoyancy was much more controlled, and I felt so much more confident in the water. The dive lasted 52 minutes with a max depth of 28.4m. The highlight came at the end during the safety stop: instead of the usual boring wait at 5m, I spotted a turtle calmly munching on corals. I kept my distance to not disturb it, but just watching it was mesmerizing.
