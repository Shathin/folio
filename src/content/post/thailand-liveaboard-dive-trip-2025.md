## Day 1 — Travel to Phuket, Thailand & Boarding the Manta Queen 7

_Date: Saturday, Nov 08, 2025_

Our journey technically began on Friday (Nov 07). We had booked a connecting flight from Bengaluru to Phuket with a long layover at Hyderabad because it was cheaper — in hindsight, a bad decision IMO 😆. The first flight was scheduled to depart at 20:10, so all of us left home about 4.5 hours early to escape the notorious Bengaluru traffic. After meeting up, we headed to the airport, reached on time, went through the usual check-in and security, and still had 1–2 hours before boarding. We grabbed dinner at the lounge, relaxed for a bit, and soon boarded our flight to Hyderabad.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_2.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Groupie before Boarding
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This is where, to me, it started feeling like a layover flight just isn’t worth it unless the cost difference is massive — which in hindsight, it wasn’t. Our next flight from Hyderabad was at 06:10 the following morning (Nov 08), about 8.5 hours later. And the worst part? We couldn’t even get our boarding passes that early, nor could we clear security or immigration. Those only open roughly 2.5–3 hours before departure. So we had an entire night to spend, but not to sleep.
We ended up staying inside the airport, trying to nap on uncomfortable chairs, watching videos, talking, eating — basically killing time however we could.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_3.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Me sleeping 😴  
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Around 02:00 AM, we tried our luck and headed toward check-in — thankfully, it was open. We collected our boarding passes, cleared security, and then walked straight into a huge immigration queue. It seemed like there was a Muslim pilgrimage event going on because a lot of people were traveling, and the lines were extremely long. The immigration officers advised us to sit and wait for the crowd to thin out, so that’s what we did.

While waiting, we unknowingly made a mistake. India now has a _[Fast Track Immigration – Trusted Travellers' Programme (FTI-TTP)](https://ftittp.mha.gov.in/)_. Prajwal decided to try signing up just to see if it worked — and he got through the process and immigration in about 15 minutes. Since the rest of us thought we'd wait to see how long it took for Prajwal, we didn’t try… and ended up waiting another 15 minutes in the regular line.

By the time we finished everything, it was ~04:15. With just an hour left before boarding began, we headed to the Hyderabad airport lounge to get some food and freshen up. Soon after, we boarded and finally set off for Phuket.

Direct flight prices at the time were ~₹25,000 (round trip), but by the time we decided, they had already shot up to around ~₹30,000 (before fees). So we picked this layover option for ~₹19,000 — a saving of ₹11,000. Fair enough. But honestly, after this experience, I’d happily pay the extra ₹11,000 next time just to avoid the fatigue, wasted time, and Air India Express reschedules (they changed timings 10–15 days before departure too, which was annoying).

We landed in Phuket on time at 12:00 (GMT+7). Thailand is 1.5 hours ahead of India. At the time of writing this, Thailand didn’t require a visa — only a Thailand Digital Arrival Certificate (TDAC), which is free and straightforward. Immigration in Phuket took a while because the queue was long, but we weren’t in a rush anyway.

Our pickup from Khao Lak Scuba Adventures (KSA) was scheduled between 15:00–16:00, so we had plenty of time. After immigration, we bought local SIM cards since we wouldn’t get another chance once we headed to Khao Lak for the liveaboard. We chilled at the airport, ate the food we’d brought from India, and waited for pickup.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_5.png" />

Our car arrived on time, but we couldn't leave immediately because one of the other divers in the group had a delayed flight and was stuck at immigration. After waiting about 45 minutes, we finally decided to leave since it was getting late. The KSA dive shop in Khao Lak was ~75 km from the airport, so the ride took around 2 hours — most of which we spent asleep.

We reached KSA around 17:30 and quickly got into the formalities. I finally met Pallava (aka Panda), the person who helped us with our booking. Then we met Lin, our lead dive guide, who walked us through the forms and helped us try out our rental gear for the liveaboard.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_6.png" />

Once everything was done, we headed to the pier — our boat, **Manta Queen 7**, was waiting for us. The crew handled all the luggage while we boarded and gathered on the main deck. They welcomed us with a drink that tasted a bit diluted but refreshing enough after the long day.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_7.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    The welcome drink
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Lin gave us our initial briefing. She introduced the entire crew — the captain, the boys who’d be helping with tanks and gear, the dive guides for each group, and of course, the cooks who would be feeding us for the next four days. She also explained the layout of the boat so we’d know our way around.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_8.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Briefing
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The boat had three levels (technically four).

- The bottom-most was the engine room — totally off-limits.
- Level 0 had the dive deck, kitchen, common bathrooms, and our ensuite cabins (each with an attached bathroom).
- Level 1 had the captain’s cabin in the front, a common room in the middle, non-ensuite cabins, and the dining/briefing area at the back.
- Level 3 was the rooftop deck with bean bags and mattresses — perfect for relaxing between dives.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_9.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Our cabin
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After the briefing, we checked out our cabins — small but cosy — and then went back up for dinner. The food was amazing! The cooks even prepared vegan options for the non-meat eaters in our group, which was really thoughtful.

<figure class="flex flex-col items-center mx-auto md:w-3/4">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/thailand-liveaboard-dive-trip-2025/day-1/1_10.png" />
  <figcaption class="text-center text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400 mt-2">
    Dinner 🍽️
  </figcaption>
</figure>

As per tradition, the crew lit a few crackers before departure — they believe it brings good luck for the journey. After the mini ceremony, we headed to our cabins, unpacked, set up our things for the next day, and got ready to sleep.

By then, the boat had already begun its journey toward the Similan Islands. Even though it was rocking slightly, I knocked out completely once I lay down. I didn’t feel a thing — slept like a log — and woke up the next morning at 05:30 without an alarm. Easily one of the best sleeps I’ve ever had.
